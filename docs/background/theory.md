# Background & Theory

## The Motivation for Interpretable Stylometry

Vector representations generated by deep neural networks (such as BERT, RoBERTa, or sentence-transformers) achieve impressive benchmark numbers across NLP tasks. However, they suffer from two major limitations in stylometry and authorship attribution:

1. **Lack of Innate Explainability**: Dense embedding dimensions are learned via latent weight optimization. Individual dimensions have no intrinsic linguistic meaning. In legal, forensic, or investigative authorship attribution, an analyst cannot justify a prediction by stating that *“dimension 412 had a value of +0.87.”*
2. **Conflation of Content and Style**: Neural embeddings primarily encode lexical semantics (the *topic* or *content* of what is said). In contrast, stylometry is fundamentally concerned with *how* something is written—syntactic structures, grammatical habits, and morphological patterns that persist across different topics.

Gram2Vec was engineered to resolve both challenges: every dimension maps directly to a concrete, interpretable linguistic feature.

## Normalization & Relative Frequencies

To ensure that feature counts are invariant to document length, Gram2Vec computes **relative frequencies**:

$$
\text{freq}(f, D) = \frac{\text{count}(f, D)}{N(D)}
$$

where:
* $\text{count}(f, D)$ is the occurrence count of feature $f$ in document $D$.
* $N(D)$ is the normalizing denominator (typically the total token count of $D$, or the sentence count for sentence-level structures).

This yields a dense feature vector of continuous normalized scores in $\mathbb{R}^k$.

---

## Dependency Tree Linearization

A key innovation in Gram2Vec is extracting complex syntactic constructions using **linearized dependency trees**. 

Rather than relying solely on surface n-grams, Gram2Vec traverses the hierarchical spaCy dependency tree and linearizes it into bracketed strings:

```text
[ROOT:VERB [nsubj:NOUN] [obj:NOUN [amod:ADJ]]]
```

A specialized regular-expression engine ([`SyntaxRegexMatcher`](../reference/api.md#gram2vec.matcher.SyntaxRegexMatcher)) can then match recurring syntactic tree templates (such as cleft sentences, passive voice constructions, or relative clauses) that characterize individual writing styles.
